# Reporting

## What to say

Report facts and what changed. Leave out the narration.

- Say what you did, what it changed, and what is blocked.
- Numbers beat adjectives. `6 of 12 furnaces working` beats `going well`.
- If something failed, quote what it actually printed.
- Never say verified or working for something you only compiled or smoke tested.
- No dashes and no semicolons. Short plain sentences. No emoji.

## What not to say

Do not recite a list JJ can read himself. Do not restate his question back at him. Do not
explain what you are about to do before doing it.

A report he reads twice has failed. So has one that buried something.

## Scheduled reports

A report on a timer is read at a glance or it is muted, and a muted report is the same as no
report.

- Hard cap of 20 lines. Fewer is better.
- One line per important item, saying what it is and what it needs.
- One line total for everything unimportant, senders named inline. Never one line each.
- If nothing matters, the whole report is one sentence saying so.
- No closing summary and no notes section unless something genuinely needs his judgement.

## Read state

Do not report something JJ has already read. On 2026 08 29 three reports in a row led with the
same three GitHub notices he had read the day before, which pushed a Khan Academy test due the
next day out of sight. See `lessons/read-state.md`.
